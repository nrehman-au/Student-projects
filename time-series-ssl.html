Category 2 Self-supervised representation learning for time series

Learning representations from unlabelled sensor, health and energy data

Industrial and healthcare time series are abundant, but labels often require a technician, clinician or field inspection. Self-supervised learning creates a training signal from the recordings themselves, then tests whether a small downstream head can solve a labelled task with less supervision.

Three ideas to understand

Contrastive learning aligns valid views of the same recording. Masked modelling reconstructs hidden samples or patches. Latent prediction estimates hidden representations instead of raw values and requires explicit collapse monitoring. Possible MSc contributions include view or mask designs based on the physics or physiology of the signal.

Learn first and find ideas

  • Methods with code: TS-TCC, TS2Vec, TF-C and SimMTM.
  • Data sources: UCI Machine Learning Repository, PhysioNet, UCR/UEA archive, Monash forecasting archive and Zenodo.
  • Monitor embedding variance and covariance from the first epoch in latent-predictive work. A smooth pretraining loss does not establish that the representation is useful.

2.1 Tetra Pak industrial collaboration: Regime-aware self-supervised learning for non-stationary filtration cycles

Project settingIndustrial collaboration with Tetra Pak; partner-supplied data under an approved agreement TaskSelf-supervised multivariate time-series learning followed by cycle-level regression or anomaly detection Recommended levelBSc or MSc; scope confirmed after an initial data profile ComputeLight to moderate; compact models on a 4-8 GB GPU Starting paperYue et al., TS2Vec, AAAI 2022

What you will do. Membrane-filtration plants generate repeated production and cleaning cycles containing flow, pressure, temperature and energy measurements. Earlier work found substantial cycle-to-cycle differences. The student will identify whether these reflect operating settings, process phases, fouling, sensor drift or abnormal operation, then learn representations that account for the resulting regimes.

Project goal. Test whether regime-aware self-supervised pretraining improves low-label prediction and transfer. The preferred task is early prediction of final specific energy consumption, such as electrical energy per unit of permeate or product. If that target cannot be constructed, use regime-conditioned anomaly detection.

Data. Tetra Pak will provide anonymised process data or an approved secure environment; the data are not public. Sources may include customer production for one product, pilot-plant trials or a new installation. Requested signals include flow, pressure, temperature, power or energy, setpoints, product/line/phase identifiers, cycle boundaries and available alarms or cleaning records. The current estimate is a few months of approximately minute-level data; the exact installation, channels and usable-cycle count must be confirmed.

Methods and starting code. Start with TS2Vec and code, TS-TCC and code, or SimMTM and code, using a compact 1-D CNN, TCN or GRU. The MSc method should make positive pairs or masks aware of cycle phase and regime, or condition the encoder on product, line and setpoints. Use change-point detection, clustering and PCA/UMAP for the initial regime analysis.

Required baselines. Regime-specific mean or running energy extrapolation; XGBoost or linear regression on cycle-prefix features; supervised TCN/GRU; generic SSL across all cycles; and a per-regime or regime-conditioned model. Include random-frozen, frozen-probe, fine-tuned and fully supervised controls when labels support them.

Evaluation. Split by complete cycle and preserve time order; never place windows from one cycle in both training and test sets. For energy regression, report MAE, RMSE and normalized MAE when predicting final specific energy from fixed prefixes such as 20%, 30% and 50%. Test a held-out period, line, product or regime where possible. For anomalies, use AUPRC plus precision and recall at an agreed alert budget. Include label-efficiency budgets and at least three seeds where sample size permits.

Bachelor scope. Profile and segment cycles, identify operating regimes, implement one supervised baseline and one established SSL method, and evaluate one target. Compare a global model with a regime-specific or conditioned variant and include one process-aware view or masking ablation. No new architecture is required.

Master research question and contribution. Research question: Can cycle-phase and operating-regime-aware self-supervised views separate legitimate cycle variation from energy inefficiency or abnormal behaviour better than generic augmentations? Compare global, per-regime and conditioned encoders at matched capacity; define positives or masks using process context; and test label efficiency plus held-out-regime transfer. The contribution is the regime-aware representation and analysis, not an unchanged TS2Vec or TS-TCC run.

Practical notes. First confirm cycle boundaries, units, synchronization, missingness, regime metadata, independent-cycle count and one defensible target. Overlapping windows do not replace independent cycles. Avoid trivial full-cycle energy calculation by forecasting early or using normalized efficiency. Data and trained artefacts remain subject to the AU-Tetra Pak agreement.

2.2 Contrastive learning for wearable activity recognition

TaskSelf-supervised time-series representation learning then classification Recommended levelBSc or MSc ComputeLight; CPU or 4 GB GPU Starting paperEldele et al., TS-TCC, IJCAI 2021

What you will do. A phone records multi-channel acceleration and angular velocity while a person walks, sits or climbs stairs. Learn an encoder from the unlabelled sensor windows, freeze it and ask whether a small classifier can recognise activities with few labels.

Project goal. Test whether self-supervised representations approach a fully supervised classifier at 1%, 5%, 10% and 100% label budgets and whether physically plausible sensor views improve orientation robustness.

Data. Use UCI Human Activity Recognition: 30 subjects, six activities, 10,299 windows, 50 Hz inertial data and a subject-independent standard split. The download is about 58 MB and is CC BY 4.0.

Methods and starting code. TS-TCC with the official code, TS2Vec with official code, and optionally CA-TCC. Use the same 1-D CNN or small Transformer encoder in SSL and supervised comparisons.

Required baselines. Random frozen encoder, SSL frozen linear probe, SSL fine-tuning and fully supervised end-to-end training. A simple feature-based classifier is a useful sanity check.

Evaluation. Accuracy and macro-F1 on subject-independent splits, label-efficiency curves and at least three seeds. For robustness, jointly rotate accelerometer and gyroscope triads at test time and measure degradation.

Bachelor scope. Reproduce TS-TCC or TS2Vec, compare frozen and fine-tuned representations with supervised training and produce the full label-efficiency curve.

Master research question and contribution. Research question: Do physics-consistent SO(3) orientation views improve label efficiency and robustness more than signal-agnostic jitter, scaling and permutation? Separate gravity from body acceleration, rotate coupled sensor triads consistently, and ablate each step. The insight comes from physically valid view construction, not tuning augmentation strength.

Practical notes. The dataset is small and includes a standard subject-independent split. Preserve subject grouping; never split windows from the same subject across train and test.

2.3 Lead-aware masked learning for 12-lead ECG

TaskSelf-supervised multivariate time-series learning then multi-label classification Recommended levelMSc preferred; BSc comparator variant ComputeModerate; 8 GB GPU recommended Starting paperDong et al., SimMTM, NeurIPS 2023

What you will do. A 12-lead ECG observes the same cardiac event from different electrical viewpoints. Instead of masking arbitrary time patches, the model hides complete leads or anatomical lead groups and predicts their latent representations from the visible leads.

Project goal. Compare contrastive and masked/latent-predictive pretraining under low-label conditions and determine whether lead-aware masking learns cross-lead physiology rather than local interpolation.

Data. Use PTB-XL: 21,799 ten-second ECGs from 18,869 patients, available at 100 and 500 Hz under CC BY 4.0 without credentialing. Use folds 1–8 for training, 9 for validation and 10 for testing; these are patient-aware.

Methods and starting code. Begin with SimMTM official code, TF-C with official code, or TS2Vec. A 1-D ResNet or compact Transformer is the shared encoder. JEPA-style work is an MSc extension and must be described as based on recent preprints rather than an established benchmark.

Required baselines. Random frozen encoder, frozen probe, fine-tuning and the same encoder fully supervised. Compare random temporal masking with complete-lead and anatomical-group masking.

Evaluation. Macro-AUROC on fold 10 for the five diagnostic superclasses is the recommended primary endpoint; add macro-AUPRC for imbalance. Report 1/10/100% label budgets, multiple seeds and patient-level splits. Monitor embedding variance and covariance during latent-predictive training.

Bachelor scope. Reproduce one stable masked method such as SimMTM against TS2Vec and supervised training on the five superclasses. JEPA is not required.

Master research question and contribution. Research question: Can a lead-identity-conditioned predictor reconstruct the latent representation of missing leads without collapse, and does that improve low-label diagnosis? Develop lead/group masking, predictor conditioning and collapse diagnostics; ablate masking granularity, EMA schedule and predictor depth. Cross-dataset transfer is optional only after the core study works.

Practical notes. This is methodologically risky because structured masking can collapse. Start at 100 Hz and five superclasses. Keep patient grouping and class definitions identical across methods.

2.4 Self-supervised forecasting representations for energy series

TaskSelf-supervised time-series learning then temporal regression Recommended levelBSc or MSc ComputeLight to moderate; 4–8 GB GPU Starting paperWoo et al., CoST, ICLR 2022

What you will do. Pretrain an encoder on unlabelled energy or transformer measurements, freeze it and fit a small forecasting head. The core comparison asks whether a reusable representation competes with a purpose-built supervised forecaster when labels or trainable parameters are restricted.

Project goal. Evaluate representation quality across several forecast horizons and determine whether season/trend-aware positive pairs help under low-label or transfer settings.

Data. Primary benchmark: ETT from the official ETDataset repository, using ETTh1/ETTh2 or ETTm1/ETTm2 with chronological splits. Optional external-validity set: UCI household power consumption, about 2.1 million minute readings from one household. It supports chronological forecasting, not household-level classification or cross-household claims.

Methods and starting code. CoST, TS2Vec and SimMTM. Use DLinear and PatchTST as supervised reference models. A ridge or linear head on frozen representations is the key diagnostic.

Required baselines. Seasonal-naive forecast, DLinear, PatchTST, random frozen encoder, frozen SSL head and SSL fine-tuning. Use identical input lengths and chronological splits.

Evaluation. MSE and MAE at agreed horizons such as 24/48/168/336/720 where the dataset supports them. Report label/data-efficiency curves, three seeds and training cost. Beating PatchTST is not a project requirement; analyse why the methods differ.

Bachelor scope. Reproduce CoST or TS2Vec on one ETT subset and compare a frozen linear head with DLinear/PatchTST. Add one second horizon or dataset only after the pipeline is stable.

Master research question and contribution. Research question: Do decomposition-aligned positive pairs improve transfer and low-data forecasting when seasonal phase drifts? Use a stable period or seasonal decomposition to select positives, compare it with random cropping and frequency-domain contrast, and analyse failure on non-stationary intervals. A negative result is useful if it reveals when estimated seasonality is unreliable.

Practical notes. Period estimation can become the whole thesis. Validate on clean ETT seasonality before using irregular household behaviour. Fit decomposition and normalization on training time only.

2.5 Self-supervised learning on heat-pump smart-meter data

TaskTime-series representation learning then household-level classification Recommended levelBSc or MSc, subject to a licence and data check ComputeLight to moderate after preprocessing Starting paperBrudermueller et al., HEAPO dataset paper, 2025

What you will do. Pretrain on a large pool of unlabelled household electricity streams, then use a small labelled subset to classify heat-pump type or operational faults. Candidate extensions can define views that account for weather and thermostatic cycling.

Project goal. Determine whether pretraining on unlabelled control households improves label efficiency on the 214 treatment households with smart-meter data and inspection information.

Data. Use HEAPO on Zenodo, with 1,408 households, and the official MIT-licensed code/dataloader. The paper reports 214 treatment and 1,194 control households plus 410 inspection protocols. The Zenodo Rights metadata does not display a dataset licence as of 16 September 2026; the paper’s CC BY 4.0 licence does not automatically license the data. Confirm reuse terms with the depositor before starting the project.

Methods and starting code. TS2Vec, TS-TCC and SimMTM or another masked model, optionally weather-conditioned. Use a 1-D CNN, InceptionTime or LSTM as the supervised model. Start with heat-pump type or a sufficiently prevalent protocol label.

Required baselines. Random frozen encoder, frozen SSL probe, SSL fine-tuning and end-to-end supervised training on identical grouped splits. Weather-only and metadata-only baselines help determine what the load series adds.

Evaluation. Group by household. Use balanced accuracy and macro-F1 for heat-pump type; macro-F1 and per-label AUROC/AUPRC for faults. Plot 1/5/10/100% label budgets. Disaggregation is a stretch only after counting households with a dedicated heat-pump meter; use nMAE/nRMSE if the subset is adequate.

Bachelor scope. After the licence check, choose one classification task, one SSL encoder and one supervised baseline. The deliverable is grouped evaluation and a label-efficiency curve.

Master research question and contribution. Research question: Do outdoor-temperature-matched positives and whole-cycle masking learn heat-pump behaviour more effectively than generic jitter and random masking? Implement one or both physics-aware views, ablate weather conditioning and compare across household groups. Disaggregation should not be the core question unless the submetered cohort is large enough.

Practical notes. Before starting, verify the dataset licence, field definitions, label prevalence, missingness and the number of households with a dedicated heat-pump meter. If reuse terms cannot be clarified, select another dataset.

2.6 Graph self-supervised learning from vibration signals

TaskGraph-level classification with cross-condition transfer Recommended levelMSc preferred; BSc supervised variant ComputeModerate preprocessing; small models on 4–8 GB GPU Starting paperHou et al., GraphMAE, KDD 2022

What you will do. Convert bearing vibration windows into graphs, pretrain a graph encoder without fault labels and test whether the representation transfers across load or speed conditions better than a raw-signal CNN.

Project goal. Separate two effects: whether the graph construction is useful and whether self-supervision adds value once that graph is fixed.

Data. Reproduction set: CWRU Bearing Data Center. MSc set: Paderborn KAt with 32 bearings, four operating conditions and 64 kHz vibration plus speed/torque/load channels; downloads. Paderborn is about 5 GB and is documented as CC BY-NC 4.0 for non-commercial academic use in the source publication.

Methods and starting code. GraphMAE or BGRL on window graphs, a supervised GNN on the same graphs and a 1-D CNN on raw vibration. Use order tracking, envelope spectra and coherence for the MSc physics graph.

Required baselines. Raw-signal 1-D CNN, supervised GNN on the exact same graph, random frozen graph encoder and SSL frozen/fine-tuned encoders. Compare physics-grounded edges with naive similarity and random edges.

Evaluation. Accuracy and macro-F1. Report in-distribution performance, but make cross-operating-condition transfer decisive: train on one condition and test on another using bearing-level grouping and identical held-out conditions for every method.

Bachelor scope. Use CWRU to build a supervised GNN fault classifier with a simple similarity graph, compare it with a 1-D CNN and randomize the edges once.

Master research question and contribution. Research question: Does a graph built from order-tracked fault-frequency sub-bands and spectral coherence transfer better across operating conditions than an ad hoc similarity graph? Compute BPFO/BPFI/BSF from bearing geometry and shaft speed, define a defensible thresholding rule, then evaluate supervised and SSL encoders. The signal-processing graph is the contribution; GraphMAE is a baseline.

Practical notes. This project requires substantial signal preprocessing. Confirm the 5 GB Paderborn download before work begins. Split by bearing, not by windows; otherwise neighbouring samples from the same physical bearing will leak across sets.
