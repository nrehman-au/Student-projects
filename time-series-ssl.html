Category 2 Self-supervised representation learning for time series

Learning representations from unlabelled sensor, health and energy data

A time series is an ordered sequence of measurements. A wearable device may record acceleration every fraction of a second, an ECG contains voltage measurements from several leads, and an industrial plant records pressure, flow, temperature and energy over time. These signals contain repeated patterns, trends and operating phases, but useful labels—such as a person’s activity, a diagnosis or an abnormal production cycle—may require expert work and are often scarce.

Self-supervised learning (SSL) learns from the unlabelled recordings by creating its own training task. A model might make two valid views of the same signal agree, hide part of a sequence and predict it, or predict the representation of a future segment. The resulting encoder converts a recording or window into a compact numerical representation. A small classification or regression head is then trained for a downstream task.

The important question is not whether the pretraining loss decreases, but whether the learned representation reduces the amount of labelled data needed and transfers to new people, machines or operating conditions. Every project therefore compares SSL with a fully supervised model and a randomly initialised encoder on leakage-safe splits.

Three ideas to understand

Contrastive learning aligns valid views of the same recording. Masked modelling reconstructs hidden samples or patches. Latent prediction estimates hidden representations instead of raw values and requires explicit collapse monitoring. Possible MSc contributions include view or mask designs based on the physics or physiology of the signal.

Learn first and find ideas

  • Methods with code: TS-TCC, TS2Vec, TF-C and SimMTM.
  • Data sources: UCI Machine Learning Repository, PhysioNet, UCR/UEA archive, Monash forecasting archive and Zenodo.
  • Monitor embedding variance and covariance from the first epoch in latent-predictive work. A smooth pretraining loss does not establish that the representation is useful.

2.1 Tetra Pak industrial collaboration: Regime-aware self-supervised learning for non-stationary filtration cycles

TaskSelf-supervised multivariate time-series learning, then cycle-level regression or anomaly detection LevelMSc only ComputeLight to moderate; compact models on a 4–8 GB GPU DataPartner-supplied by Tetra Pak under an approved agreement; not public Starting paperYue et al., TS2Vec, AAAI 2022

What you will do. Membrane-filtration plants generate repeated production and cleaning cycles containing flow, pressure, temperature and energy measurements. Cycle-to-cycle differences are large. You will learn representations that account for the plant’s operating regimes, then test whether they predict an operational target better than representations that ignore regime.

Data. Tetra Pak provides anonymised process data or an approved secure environment. Requested signals include flow, pressure, temperature, power or energy, setpoints, product/line/phase identifiers, cycle boundaries and available alarms or cleaning records. Expect a few months of roughly minute-level data. Split by complete cycle and preserve time order; overlapping windows are not independent cycles.

Methods and code. TS2Vec, TS-TCC or SimMTM, with a compact 1-D CNN, TCN or GRU encoder. Change-point detection, clustering and PCA/UMAP support the initial regime analysis.

Baselines and evaluation. Compare a regime-specific statistical baseline, a supervised TCN/GRU, generic SSL across all cycles and a regime-aware or regime-conditioned encoder, with random-frozen and frozen-probe controls. Primary metric is MAE for energy regression or AUPRC for anomaly scoring, reported with label-efficiency budgets and at least three seeds.

Master research question. Can cycle-phase- and operating-regime-aware self-supervised views separate legitimate process variation from energy inefficiency or abnormal behaviour better than generic augmentations? The contribution is the process-aware view or masking design plus a matched-budget comparison against generic augmentation, including transfer to a held-out regime, line or product. Rerunning an unchanged TS2Vec or TS-TCC is a reproduction, not the contribution.

Practical notes. Confirm cycle boundaries, units, synchronisation, missingness, regime metadata, independent-cycle count and one defensible target before modelling. Data and trained artefacts remain subject to the AU–Tetra Pak agreement.

2.2 Contrastive learning for wearable activity recognition

TaskSelf-supervised time-series representation learning, then classification LevelBSc only ComputeLight; CPU or 4 GB GPU DataUCI Human Activity Recognition — 30 subjects, 6 activities, 10,299 windows, 50 Hz, CC BY 4.0 Starting paperEldele et al., TS-TCC, IJCAI 2021

What you will do. A phone records acceleration and angular velocity while a person walks, sits or climbs stairs. Learn an encoder from the unlabelled sensor windows, freeze it, and measure how few labels a small classifier needs to recognise activities.

Data. UCI HAR, about 58 MB, with a standard subject-independent split. Preserve subject grouping: never place windows from the same subject in both training and test sets.

Methods and code. TS-TCC or TS2Vec — choose one. Use the same 1-D CNN or small Transformer encoder for the self-supervised and supervised comparisons.

Baselines and evaluation. Random frozen encoder, SSL frozen linear probe, SSL fine-tuning and a fully supervised model trained from scratch. Report accuracy and macro-F1 on the subject-independent split.

Bachelor research question. How much labelled activity data can self-supervised pretraining replace?

Core experiment. Produce a full label-efficiency curve at 1%, 5%, 10%, 25% and 100% of the labels, for all four conditions above. At the 1% and 5% budgets, resample which labels are used across at least five subject-stratified draws and report confidence intervals — a single 1% sample can come from only one or two subjects, which would make that end of the curve meaningless.

The deliverable is a statement such as: with 10% of the labels, self-supervised pretraining reaches X% of the performance achieved with all labels.

As an extension, test robustness to sensor orientation by jointly rotating the accelerometer and gyroscope triads at test time and measuring the degradation.

2.3 Lead-aware masked learning for 12-lead ECG

TaskSelf-supervised multivariate time-series learning, then multi-label classification LevelMSc only — a reduced Bachelor version is possible ComputeModerate; 8 GB GPU recommended DataPTB-XL — 21,799 ECGs, 18,869 patients, 100/500 Hz, CC BY 4.0, no credentialing Starting paperDong et al., SimMTM, NeurIPS 2023

What you will do. A 12-lead ECG observes the same cardiac event from different electrical viewpoints. Instead of masking arbitrary time patches, the model hides complete leads or anatomical lead groups and predicts their latent representations from the visible leads.

Data. PTB-XL at 100 Hz, using the official patient-aware folds: 1–8 for training, 9 for validation, 10 for testing. The dataset is fully labelled, so label budgets simulate scarcity rather than reflecting missing annotations.

Methods and code. SimMTM, TF-C or TS2Vec, with a 1-D ResNet or compact Transformer as the shared encoder.

Baselines and evaluation. Random frozen encoder, frozen probe, fine-tuning and the same encoder fully supervised. Macro-AUROC on fold 10 across the five diagnostic superclasses is the primary endpoint; add macro-AUPRC for class imbalance. Report patient-level splits and several label budgets.

Master research question. Can a lead-identity-conditioned predictor reconstruct the latent representation of missing leads without collapse, and does that improve low-label diagnosis? Develop complete-lead and anatomical-group masking, predictor conditioning and collapse diagnostics, then ablate masking granularity, EMA schedule and predictor depth. This route is methodologically risky: structured masking can collapse, so monitor embedding variance and covariance from the first epoch.

Reduced Bachelor version. Requires a supplied PTB-XL dataloader and a shared encoder. Choose one of two questions.

(a) Contrastive learning or masked modelling: which objective produces better ECG representations? Implement both objectives on one identical encoder. Comparing two published repositories instead would confound the objective with the encoder architecture, the augmentation settings and how much each implementation was already tuned, so no conclusion about contrastive versus masked learning would follow. Only the pretext task may differ.

(b) How many ECG leads do you actually need? Train and evaluate using 12 leads, 6 leads, 3 leads and lead II alone, and measure how diagnostic performance falls as leads are removed. This is directly relevant to single-lead wearable devices, needs one codebase, and establishes which leads carry the diagnostic signal that the Master route then exploits.

2.4 Self-supervised forecasting representations for energy series

TaskSelf-supervised time-series learning, then temporal regression LevelBSc or MSc ComputeLight to moderate; 4–8 GB GPU DataETT — electricity transformer temperature, hourly (ETTh1/ETTh2) and 15-minute (ETTm1/ETTm2) subsets Starting paperWoo et al., CoST, ICLR 2022

What you will do. Pretrain an encoder on one unlabelled energy series, freeze it, and fit a small forecasting head. The core comparison asks whether a reusable representation transfers to a different series, or whether a purpose-built supervised forecaster trained directly on that series is simply better.

Data. Use ETT with chronological splits, fitting scalers on the training period only. ETTh1 and ETTh2 come from two different transformers, which provides genuine distribution shift; ETTh1 to ETTm1 additionally changes the sampling rate.

Methods and code. CoST, TS2Vec or SimMTM for pretraining. DLinear is the required supervised reference; PatchTST is optional. A ridge or linear head on frozen representations is the key diagnostic.

Baselines and evaluation. Seasonal-naive, DLinear, frozen SSL representation with a ridge head, and SSL fine-tuning. Report MSE and MAE at agreed horizons with identical input lengths and chronological splits, plus three seeds.

Bachelor research question. Do self-supervised representations pretrained on one energy series transfer to a different one?

Core experiment. Pretrain the encoder once on ETTh1, then probe and fine-tune it on ETTh2, comparing against the same encoder pretrained directly on ETTh2 and against DLinear trained on ETTh2. An ETTh1-to-ETTm1 variant additionally tests transfer across sampling rates. Add a second dataset only after the first pipeline is stable.

Expected outcome. DLinear is a very strong baseline on ETT and may beat the self-supervised pipeline outright. That is a valid and useful finding for this project — report it and analyse why, rather than tuning until the representation appears to win.

Master research question. Do decomposition-aligned positive pairs improve transfer and low-data forecasting when seasonal phase drifts? Use a stable period or seasonal decomposition to select positives, compare with random cropping and frequency-domain contrast, and analyse failure on non-stationary intervals. A negative result is useful if it identifies when estimated seasonality is unreliable. Period estimation can consume the whole thesis, so validate on clean ETT seasonality first.

2.5 Self-supervised learning on heat-pump smart-meter data

TaskTime-series representation learning, then household-level classification LevelMSc only — a reduced Bachelor version is possible ComputeLight to moderate after preprocessing DataHEAPO on Zenodo — 1,408 Swiss households, 15-minute and daily resolution, 2018–2024, CC BY 4.0 Starting paperBrudermueller et al., HEAPO, 2025

What you will do. HEAPO contains a large pool of households with smart-meter data but no inspection labels, and a much smaller pool with both. You will pretrain on the unlabelled households and test whether that improves classification on the small labelled group.

Data. 214 treatment households have smart-meter data and inspection information, 1,194 control households have no labels, and there are 410 inspection protocols, plus household metadata and weather data from 8 stations. Use the official MIT-licensed dataloader rather than writing a new pipeline. Group all splits by household, and verify label prevalence and class balance before fixing the task.

Methods and code. TS2Vec, TS-TCC or a masked model such as SimMTM, with a 1-D CNN, InceptionTime or LSTM as the supervised comparison. Start from heat-pump type or another sufficiently prevalent label.

Baselines and evaluation. Random frozen encoder, frozen SSL probe, SSL fine-tuning and end-to-end supervised training on identical household-grouped splits. Weather-only and metadata-only baselines show what the load series adds. Report balanced accuracy and macro-F1 with confidence intervals.

Master research question. Can unlabelled data from a large related household population improve prediction on a much smaller labelled population, and do weather- and cycle-aware views improve it further? Compare pretraining sources — none, labelled households only, unlabelled control households, and all households — then add outdoor-temperature-matched positives or whole-cycle masking and ablate the weather conditioning.

A note on statistical power. There are only 214 labelled households, so a grouped test fold holds roughly 40–70 of them and confidence intervals on accuracy are wide — wider than the improvement self-supervised pretraining typically produces. Use repeated grouped cross-validation, decide the single primary comparison in advance rather than testing all four pretraining sources against each other, and report confidence intervals throughout. A carefully executed study that concludes the difference cannot be resolved at this sample size is a valid outcome for this project.

Reduced Bachelor version. Use the official dataloader, pick the most prevalent and balanced label, and compare two pretraining sources — none versus unlabelled control households — on household-grouped splits, with a label-efficiency curve. The power note above applies equally.

2.6 Graph self-supervised learning from vibration signals

TaskGraph-level classification (one graph per signal window) with cross-operating-condition transfer LevelMSc only ComputeModerate preprocessing; small models on a 4–8 GB GPU DataPaderborn KAt (~5 GB, CC BY-NC 4.0) for the main study; CWRU for pipeline development Starting paperWang et al., graph representation via wavelet packet decomposition with graph contrastive pretraining, 2025

What you will do. A vibration window has no natural graph — you construct one. Each window becomes its own graph whose nodes are frequency bands fixed by bearing geometry and whose edges measure which of those bands are modulated together in that window. You pretrain a graph encoder without fault labels, then test whether the representation transfers to operating conditions it never saw during training.

How the graph is built. This is the core design decision, so it is specified rather than left open:

  1. Order-domain resampling. Resample each window from uniform time steps to uniform shaft-angle steps (computed order tracking), so the spectrum axis becomes orders — cycles per shaft revolution — instead of Hz.
  2. Fixed node vocabulary. Bearing characteristic frequencies scale with shaft speed, but their ratios to shaft speed are pure geometry and therefore speed-invariant. Define nodes as order bands centred on BPFO, BPFI, BSF and FTF and their harmonics, plus shaft harmonics and a set of broadband bands — roughly 30–40 nodes. Node identity is then the same in every window, at every speed. Give the bands finite width (about ±1.5%) because rolling-element slip shifts real fault components off their theoretical orders.
  3. Node features, per window. Band energy, envelope-spectrum amplitude at that order, spectral kurtosis, band RMS.
  4. Edges, per window. A fault produces a periodic impact train that rings several structural resonances at once, so the envelopes of those bands rise and fall together. Measure that with envelope correlation between band-filtered components, or with cyclic spectral coherence. Restrict candidate edges to a physics backbone — harmonics within a fault family, and carrier-to-sideband pairs (BPFI ± shaft order, BSF ± FTF) — then let the window’s data set the weights, and keep the top-k per node.

So the node set is fixed across the dataset while features and edge weights are recomputed for every window — the co-modulation pattern is the diagnostic signal.

Data. Paderborn KAt (32 bearings, four operating conditions, 64 kHz vibration plus recorded speed, torque and radial load) is the main dataset, via the download page; its recorded speed makes order tracking straightforward. CWRU is for building the pipeline. Split by bearing, never by window.

Methods and code. A GIN encoder on the window graphs, pretrained with GraphCL (contrastive) or GraphMAE (masked feature reconstruction) — both are graph-level methods, unlike node-level approaches such as BGRL. Note that the standard GraphCL augmentations need care here: dropping the BPFO 2× node does not perturb the graph, it deletes the evidence.

Baselines and evaluation. Two mandatory non-graph baselines: envelope-order-spectrum features with a gradient-boosted classifier, and cyclic spectral coherence maps with a CNN — the established domain method, and the one a graph result has to beat to mean anything. Then a four-arm factorial on identical graphs: physics backbone with per-window weights; the same backbone with weights fixed equal; random edges; and no edges. Accuracy and macro-F1, with cross-operating-condition transfer as the primary endpoint — train on one load/speed condition, test on another, with identical held-out conditions for every method. Fit thresholds and normalisation on training data only.

Master research question. Does a speed-invariant, kinematics-derived graph representation of a vibration window transfer across operating conditions better than generic time-frequency graphs — and is any gain due to the graph construction, the self-supervised pretraining, or both?

Existing graph-based bearing diagnosis defines nodes from generic decompositions — wavelet-packet bands or spectrogram bins — which are not comparable across shaft speeds. Defining the node vocabulary from bearing kinematics in the order domain makes the graph structurally invariant to speed, which is what should allow representations to transfer. Testing that is the contribution; GraphCL and GraphMAE are baselines.

What the comparison tests. With a fixed node set, every node’s features could be concatenated into one vector and passed to gradient boosting, using all the marginal band information. The graph adds value only if which bands co-modulate carries information the individual band features do not. The four arms test this. The fixed-weight arm is the most informative: if the adjacency were identical for every window, the GNN would reduce to a fixed linear operator over node features — a structured MLP rather than graph learning.

Why in-distribution accuracy will not answer this. CWRU saturates near 99–100% for almost any method, including simple hand-crafted features. If all four arms score within a point of each other, the comparison has resolved nothing. Cross-condition transfer moves performance into a range where the arms can be separated.

Related work to start from. A 2025 review of GNNs for rolling bearing fault diagnosis maps the field into temporal, feature and spatiotemporal graphs. For the closest existing constructions, see wavelet-packet bands as nodes with correlation-based edges and graph contrastive pretraining, a GCN over constant-Q spectrogram bins with fundamental-to-harmonic edges, and a physics-informed GCN deriving edge weights from bearing dynamics. A graph-level representation-learning paper argues against the more common sample-as-node paradigm. For the signal processing, see a review of diagnosis under time-varying speeds and Abboud et al. on envelope analysis in variable-speed conditions.
