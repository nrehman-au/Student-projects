Category 2 Self-supervised representation learning for time series

Learning representations from unlabelled sensor, health and energy data

A time series is an ordered sequence of measurements. A wearable device may record acceleration every fraction of a second, an ECG contains voltage measurements from several leads, and an industrial plant records pressure, flow, temperature and energy over time. These signals contain repeated patterns, trends and operating phases, but useful labels—such as a person’s activity, a diagnosis or an abnormal production cycle—may require expert work and are often scarce.

Self-supervised learning (SSL) learns from the unlabelled recordings by creating its own training task. A model might make two valid views of the same signal agree, hide part of a sequence and predict it, or predict the representation of a future segment. The resulting encoder converts a recording or window into a compact numerical representation. A small classification or regression head is then trained for a downstream task.

Evaluation focuses on whether the representation reduces the amount of labelled data needed and transfers to new people, machines or operating conditions. Each project compares SSL with a fully supervised model and a randomly initialised encoder on leakage-safe splits.

Three ideas to understand

Contrastive learning aligns valid views of the same recording. Masked modelling reconstructs hidden samples or patches. Latent prediction estimates hidden representations instead of raw values and requires explicit collapse monitoring. Possible MSc contributions include view or mask designs based on the physics or physiology of the signal.

Learn first and find ideas

  • Methods with code: TS-TCC, TS2Vec, TF-C and SimMTM.
  • Data sources: UCI Machine Learning Repository, PhysioNet, UCR/UEA archive, Monash forecasting archive and Zenodo.
  • Monitor embedding variance and covariance from the first epoch in latent-predictive work. A smooth pretraining loss does not establish that the representation is useful.

2.1 Tetra Pak industrial collaboration: Regime-aware self-supervised learning for non-stationary filtration cycles

TaskSelf-supervised multivariate time-series learning, then cycle-level regression or anomaly detection LevelMSc only ComputeLight to moderate; compact models on a 4–8 GB GPU DataPartner-supplied by Tetra Pak under an approved agreement; not public Starting paperYue et al., TS2Vec, AAAI 2022

What you will do. Membrane-filtration plants generate repeated production and cleaning cycles containing flow, pressure, temperature and energy measurements. Cycle-to-cycle differences are large. You will learn representations that account for the plant’s operating regimes, then test whether they predict an operational target better than representations that ignore regime.

Data. Tetra Pak will provide anonymised process data or a secure environment; the data are not public. Required fields include cycle boundaries, flow, pressure, temperature, power or energy, setpoints and operating-regime information. Data volume, sampling rate and the downstream target must be confirmed before allocation. Split by complete cycle and preserve time order.

Methods and code. Use TS2Vec, TS-TCC or SimMTM with a compact 1-D CNN, TCN or GRU. Use change-point detection and clustering to identify regimes before pretraining.

Baselines and evaluation. Compare a statistical baseline, supervised TCN/GRU, generic SSL and a regime-aware encoder. Include random-frozen and frozen-probe controls. Use MAE for early prediction of final specific energy, or AUPRC for an agreed anomaly label. Report label-efficiency results and three seeds when the number of independent cycles permits.

Master research question. Can cycle-phase- and operating-regime-aware self-supervised views separate legitimate process variation from energy inefficiency or abnormal behaviour better than generic augmentations? The contribution is the process-aware view or masking design plus a matched-budget comparison against generic augmentation, including transfer to a held-out regime, line or product. Rerunning an unchanged TS2Vec or TS-TCC is a reproduction, not the contribution.

Practical notes. Confirm the independent-cycle count and one measurable target before allocation. Data and trained artefacts remain subject to the AU–Tetra Pak agreement.

2.2 Contrastive learning for wearable activity recognition

TaskSelf-supervised time-series representation learning, then classification LevelBSc only ComputeLight; CPU or 4 GB GPU DataUCI Human Activity Recognition — 30 subjects, 6 activities, 10,299 windows, 50 Hz, CC BY 4.0 Starting paperEldele et al., TS-TCC, IJCAI 2021

What you will do. A phone records acceleration and angular velocity while a person walks, sits or climbs stairs. Learn an encoder from the unlabelled sensor windows, freeze it, and measure how few labels a small classifier needs to recognise activities.

Data. UCI HAR is a 58 MB public download under CC BY 4.0. It contains pre-windowed 2.56-second recordings with 50% overlap and a standard subject-independent split. Keep that split.

Methods and code. TS-TCC or TS2Vec — choose one. Use the same 1-D CNN or small Transformer encoder for the self-supervised and supervised comparisons.

Baselines and evaluation. Random frozen encoder, SSL frozen linear probe, SSL fine-tuning and a fully supervised model trained from scratch. Report accuracy and macro-F1 on the subject-independent split.

Bachelor research question. How much labelled activity data can self-supervised pretraining replace?

Core experiment. Compare all four conditions at 1%, 5%, 10%, 25% and 100% label budgets. Resample the labelled windows within the training subjects and report confidence intervals. Optionally test robustness by jointly rotating the accelerometer and gyroscope axes.

2.3 Lead-aware masked learning for 12-lead ECG

TaskSelf-supervised multivariate time-series learning, then multi-label classification LevelMSc preferred; BSc with a supplied data pipeline ComputeModerate; 8 GB GPU recommended DataPTB-XL — 21,799 ECGs, 18,869 patients, 100/500 Hz, CC BY 4.0, no credentialing Starting paperDong et al., SimMTM, NeurIPS 2023

What you will do. A 12-lead ECG observes the same cardiac event from different electrical viewpoints. Instead of masking arbitrary time patches, the model hides complete leads or anatomical lead groups and predicts their latent representations from the visible leads.

Data. PTB-XL at 100 Hz, using the official patient-aware folds: 1–8 for training, 9 for validation, 10 for testing. The dataset is fully labelled, so label budgets simulate scarcity rather than reflecting missing annotations.

Methods and code. Start with SimMTM or TS2Vec and a 1-D ResNet or compact Transformer. The PTB-XL benchmark code provides preprocessing and supervised references.

Baselines and evaluation. Random frozen encoder, frozen probe, fine-tuning and the same encoder fully supervised. Macro-AUROC on fold 10 across the five diagnostic superclasses is the primary endpoint; add macro-AUPRC for class imbalance. Report patient-level splits and several label budgets.

Master research question. Does lead-aware masking improve low-label diagnosis over random temporal masking? Develop complete-lead or anatomical-group masking, condition the predictor on lead identity, and monitor embedding variance for collapse. Ablate masking granularity and predictor depth.

Bachelor question. How does diagnostic performance change when fewer ECG leads are available? With the supplied pipeline and one encoder, compare 12, 6 and 3 leads and lead II alone under supervised training and one established SSL method.

2.4 Self-supervised forecasting representations for energy series

TaskSelf-supervised time-series learning, then temporal regression LevelBSc or MSc ComputeLight to moderate; 4–8 GB GPU DataETT — electricity transformer temperature, hourly (ETTh1/ETTh2) and 15-minute (ETTm1/ETTm2) subsets Starting paperWoo et al., CoST, ICLR 2022

What you will do. Pretrain an encoder on one unlabelled energy series, freeze it, and fit a small forecasting head. Test whether the representation transfers to a different series better than training a supervised forecaster on that series.

Data. Use ETT with chronological splits and training-only scaling. ETTh1 and ETTh2 come from different transformers. The public repository uses CC BY-ND 4.0; cite it and do not redistribute modified copies.

Methods and code. CoST, TS2Vec or SimMTM for pretraining. DLinear is the required supervised reference; PatchTST is optional. A ridge or linear head on frozen representations is the key diagnostic.

Baselines and evaluation. Seasonal-naive, DLinear, frozen SSL representation with a ridge head, and SSL fine-tuning. Report MSE and MAE at agreed horizons with identical input lengths and chronological splits, plus three seeds.

Bachelor research question. Do self-supervised representations pretrained on one energy series transfer to a different one?

Core experiment. Pretrain on ETTh1, then probe and fine-tune on ETTh2. Compare with direct ETTh2 pretraining and DLinear trained on ETTh2.

Interpretation. DLinear may outperform the SSL pipeline. Analyse whether the gap changes with forecast horizon and target-data volume.

Master research question. Do decomposition-aligned positive pairs improve transfer and low-data forecasting when seasonal phase drifts? Use a stable seasonal decomposition to select positives, compare with random cropping and frequency-domain contrast, and analyse non-stationary intervals. Validate the period estimate on ETT before adding irregular data.

2.5 Self-supervised learning on heat-pump smart-meter data

TaskTime-series representation learning, then household-level classification LevelMSc preferred; BSc metadata-label version ComputeLight to moderate after preprocessing DataHEAPO on Zenodo — 1,408 Swiss households, 15-minute and daily resolution, 2018–2024; dataset licence must be confirmed Starting paperBrudermueller et al., HEAPO, 2025

What you will do. Pretrain on household electricity series, then test whether the representations improve prediction of a household attribute or an inspection-derived issue.

Data. The 458 MB public download contains 1,408 households; 214 have both smart-meter data and inspection information. Use the official MIT-licensed dataloader. The Zenodo Rights field does not state a dataset licence, so confirm reuse terms before allocation. Group all splits by household.

Methods and code. Use TS2Vec or SimMTM, with a 1-D CNN or InceptionTime supervised baseline. Use heat-pump type for the lower-risk task, or one inspection-derived issue only after counting usable positives among the 214 matched households.

Baselines and evaluation. Compare random-frozen, frozen SSL, fine-tuned SSL and supervised encoders on identical household splits. Include weather-only and metadata-only baselines. Report balanced accuracy, macro-F1 and confidence intervals using repeated grouped cross-validation.

Master research question. Can unlabelled data from a large related household population improve prediction on a much smaller labelled population, and do weather- and cycle-aware views improve it further? Compare pretraining sources — none, labelled households only, unlabelled control households, and all households — then add outdoor-temperature-matched positives or whole-cycle masking and ablate the weather conditioning.

Feasibility condition. An inspection-label study has only 214 matched households. Select one primary label and verify its prevalence before committing to it; otherwise use the better-covered heat-pump-type task.

Bachelor version. Use heat-pump type or another well-covered metadata label. Compare supervised training with one SSL encoder on household-grouped splits and report a label-efficiency curve.

2.6 Graph self-supervised learning from vibration signals

TaskGraph-level classification (one graph per signal window) with cross-operating-condition transfer LevelMSc only ComputeModerate preprocessing; small models on a 4–8 GB GPU DataPaderborn KAt — ~5 GB, 32 bearings, four operating conditions, CC BY-NC 4.0 Starting paperHou et al., GraphMAE, KDD 2022 — official code

What you will do. Convert each vibration window into a graph of fault-related frequency bands, pretrain a graph encoder without labels, and test transfer to a different load or speed condition.

Graph construction. Resample each window into the shaft-order domain. Use fixed nodes for BPFO, BPFI, BSF and FTF bands and their harmonics. Node features can include band energy, envelope amplitude, spectral kurtosis and RMS; edges can represent envelope correlation or spectral coherence. Fit thresholds on training data only.

Data. Paderborn provides 64 kHz vibration, speed, torque and radial load for 32 physical bearings under four operating conditions. Download it from the official archive. Split by bearing, not by window. CWRU may be used only to develop the preprocessing pipeline; its official site does not state a clear data licence.

Methods and code. Use a GIN encoder with GraphMAE. Compare with supervised GIN and a 1-D CNN. Do not remove fault-frequency nodes as a generic graph augmentation.

Baselines and evaluation. Compare a 1-D CNN, envelope-spectrum features with gradient boosting, supervised GIN, random-frozen GIN, and GraphMAE with frozen and fine-tuned heads. Test physics-based, similarity, random and no-edge graphs. Report accuracy and macro-F1; cross-condition transfer is primary.

Master research question. Does a speed-invariant graph built from bearing kinematics transfer across operating conditions better than a similarity graph, and does self-supervised pretraining add value once the graph is fixed? The contribution is the graph construction and controlled ablation; GraphMAE is a baseline.

Practical notes. The dataset has only 32 independent bearings. Use bearing-grouped splits, report confidence intervals and keep the graph, SSL method and transfer experiment fixed in advance. This project is not suitable for a short BSc project.
